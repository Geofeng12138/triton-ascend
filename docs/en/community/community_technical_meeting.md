# Technical Meeting

Triton-Ascend holds regular community technical meetings as the core decision-making mechanism for project governance. Major matters such as technical direction, significant proposal reviews, and version releases are discussed and decided during these meetings, with voting conducted by all `Maintainers`. The meetings are open to all community members; anyone can participate in discussions, but decisions and voting are carried out solely by `Maintainers`.

## Meeting Content

- Discussion and decision-making on technical direction
- Review and voting on major proposals (RFCs)
- Version release planning and timeline
- Prioritization of community Issues and PRs
- Discussion on contributor nomination and removal

## Voting Rules

- `Maintainers` who do not attend the meeting must submit their votes within 3 working days after the meeting; failure to vote within this period is considered abstention
- A decision is passed if more than half of the participating `Maintainers` agree (abstentions are not counted in the total votes)
- Voting results are recorded in the meeting minutes

## Decision-Making Process

### Routine Decisions

Routine decisions in daily development (such as `Bug` fixes, small feature improvements) are reviewed and decided by the `Maintainer` of the relevant module in the `Issue` or `PR`, without requiring a meeting.

### Major Decisions

The following matters are considered major decisions and require collective discussion by `Maintainers` during the community technical meeting:

- Major architectural changes
- Breaking `API` changes
- Adding core features or removing existing features
- Major upgrades of third-party dependencies
- Version release planning

Major Decision Process:

1. Submit an `RFC` (Request for Comments) describing the change content, scope of impact, and implementation plan
2. Discuss during the technical meeting
3. `Maintainers` make a collective decision
